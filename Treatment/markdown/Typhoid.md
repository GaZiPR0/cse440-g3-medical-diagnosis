# 🌡️ Typhoid Fever

> **In short:** A bacterial infection spread through contaminated food and water. The fever climbs higher day after day and needs a full course of antibiotics to clear.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Caused by** | Salmonella Typhi |
| **Spread by** | Unsafe food & water |
| **Key sign** | Step-ladder fever |
| **Treatment** | Antibiotics |

---

## Overview

**Typhoid fever** is caused by the bacterium *Salmonella Typhi*. It spreads when food or water carrying the bacteria is swallowed — usually because of poor sanitation, unsafe drinking water, or food handled by someone who is infected.

The illness has a distinctive pattern. The fever does not spike and fall like malaria; instead it rises a little more each day for about a week and then stays high. Doctors call this a **step-ladder fever**. Left untreated, typhoid can go on for weeks and lead to serious complications, but with the right antibiotics most people recover fully.

## Symptoms

- Fever that climbs higher each day and stays high
- Continuous dull pain in the stomach
- Constipation early on, sometimes followed by diarrhoea
- Flat, rose-coloured spots on the chest and abdomen
- Severe weakness, tiredness and drowsiness
- Headache and loss of appetite
- A swollen, tender abdomen

> ⚠️ **Seek care immediately if you notice:** severe stomach pain, blood in the stool, black tarry stools, persistent vomiting, confusion, or extreme drowsiness. These can signal bleeding or a perforation in the intestine, which is a medical emergency.

## Diagnosis

Typhoid is confirmed by finding the bacteria in the body. A **blood culture** is the most reliable test, especially in the first week of illness. Stool and urine cultures may be used later on. The older **Widal test** is still common in some places, but it can give false results and should not be relied on by itself.

Tell your doctor where you have been and what you have eaten or drunk recently. Typhoid looks very similar to dengue, malaria and other fevers, so this history matters.

## Treatment

Typhoid is treated with **antibiotics prescribed by a doctor**. Which one is right depends on local resistance patterns, so never self-medicate or reuse someone else's prescription.

### Finish the whole course

Fever often settles within a few days of starting antibiotics, and people feel well enough to stop. Stopping early is the main reason typhoid comes back and the main reason drug-resistant strains develop. Take every dose, right to the end.

### Supporting your recovery

- Drink plenty of safe fluids — boiled or bottled water, oral rehydration solution
- Eat small, soft, easily digested meals rather than large ones
- Rest completely; typhoid leaves the body very weak
- Use paracetamol for fever and headache
- Wash your hands after using the toilet so you do not pass it to your family

> 💡 **A note on carriers:** a small number of people continue to carry and shed the bacteria after they feel well. If your doctor asks for follow-up stool tests, do them.

## Prevention

- Drink only boiled, filtered or sealed bottled water
- Avoid ice from unknown sources
- Eat food that is freshly cooked and served hot
- Peel fruit yourself; avoid raw salads from street stalls
- Wash hands with soap before eating and after using the toilet
- Ask about the typhoid vaccine if you live in or travel to a high-risk area
