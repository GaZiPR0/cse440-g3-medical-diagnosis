# 🤧 Common Cold

> **In short:** A mild viral infection of the nose and throat. It comes on gradually, stays mild, and clears on its own within about a week.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Caused by** | Rhinovirus & others |
| **Spread by** | Droplets and touch |
| **Key sign** | Runny nose & sneezing |
| **Usually lasts** | 7–10 days |

---

## Overview

The **common cold** is a mild infection of the nose and throat caused by any of more than two hundred viruses — most often a rhinovirus. Because there are so many, and because they change constantly, catching one gives you no lasting protection against the next. Adults average two to three colds a year, children rather more.

A cold spreads through droplets in the air when someone coughs or sneezes, and through surfaces — a doorknob or a phone — touched by unwashed hands and then carried to your own nose or eyes.

## Symptoms

Symptoms build up gradually over a day or two and stay mild. That slow, mild onset is the clearest difference from influenza, which hits suddenly and hard.

- Frequent sneezing
- A runny nose with clear, watery mucus, thickening after a day or two
- A mild, scratchy sore throat
- Watering eyes
- A blocked nose
- A mild cough, and a slight fever in some people

> 💡 **Cold or flu?** a cold starts slowly and you can usually carry on with your day. Influenza starts abruptly with high fever, aching muscles and exhaustion that puts you in bed.

> ⚠️ **See a doctor if:** symptoms last beyond ten days or get worse after improving, you have a fever above 38.5°C for more than three days, you are short of breath or wheezing, or you have severe sinus or ear pain.

## Diagnosis

A cold is diagnosed from the symptoms; no test is needed. A doctor may check the throat, ears and chest to rule out a bacterial throat infection, an ear infection, or a chest infection if symptoms are unusual or drag on.

## Treatment

There is no cure, and **antibiotics do not work against a cold** — they treat bacteria, not viruses. Treatment simply makes you more comfortable while your immune system does the work.

- Rest, and drink plenty of warm fluids
- Gargle with warm salt water for a sore throat
- Use saline drops or steam inhalation for a blocked nose
- Take paracetamol for aches and fever
- Honey in warm water soothes a cough (**never give honey to a child under one year**)
- Keep the air from getting too dry

> ⚠️ **For children:** over-the-counter cold and cough medicines are not recommended under six years of age, and aspirin should never be given to a child or teenager with a viral illness.

## Prevention

- Wash hands with soap regularly, especially before eating
- Keep hands away from your eyes, nose and mouth
- Cough and sneeze into your elbow or a tissue, then wash your hands
- Do not share cups, bottles or towels
- Clean frequently touched surfaces such as phones and door handles
- Stay home when you are unwell so you do not pass it on
