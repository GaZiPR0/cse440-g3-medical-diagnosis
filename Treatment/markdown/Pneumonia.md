# 🫁 Pneumonia

> **In short:** Pneumonia is an infection that inflames the air sacs of one or both lungs, which may fill with fluid or pus. It ranges from mild to serious and is treated based on its cause and severity.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Category** | Lung infection |
| **Caused by** | Bacteria or viruses |
| **Key signs** | Cough, fever, breathlessness |
| **Most cases** | Treated at home |

---

## Overview

**Pneumonia** is a bacterial or viral infection of the lungs. It inflames the tiny air sacs (alveoli), which can fill with fluid or pus and make breathing harder. Symptoms include fever, chills, shortness of breath, a cough that produces phlegm, and chest pain. It can affect anyone, but is most serious for infants, older adults, and people with other health conditions.

## Symptoms

- Cough that produces phlegm
- Fever and chills
- Shortness of breath
- Chest pain
- Fatigue
- Sweating

## Diagnosis

Your doctor starts with your medical history and a physical exam, listening to your lungs for the abnormal bubbling or crackling sounds that suggest pneumonia. If pneumonia is suspected, tests may include:

- **Blood tests** — confirm infection and help identify the organism responsible.
- **Chest X-ray** — shows the extent and location of the infection.
- **Pulse oximetry** — measures the oxygen level in your blood.
- **Sputum test** — analyses fluid coughed up from the lungs to pinpoint the cause.

For people over 65, those in hospital, or those with severe symptoms, a doctor may also order a **CT scan** or a **pleural fluid culture**.

## Treatment

Most cases can be treated at home, but babies, children, and people with severe pneumonia may need hospital care. Pneumonia is usually treated with **antibiotics** — even when a virus is suspected, since a bacterial infection may also be present. The choice and delivery of antibiotic depend on the cause and severity.

**Home-based care** — oral antibiotics, pain relief, paracetamol for fever, plenty of fluids to loosen mucus, and rest.

**Hospital care** — intravenous antibiotics, oxygen therapy, IV fluids, and physiotherapy to help clear the lungs.

> ℹ️ **Recovery.** Full recovery can take several weeks. A lingering cough as the lungs clear mucus is normal, along with tiredness. See a doctor if the cough worsens or recovery drags beyond a few weeks. Smokers should have a chest X-ray after six weeks to confirm the lungs have cleared.

## Prevention

- Get vaccinated — especially against pneumococcal disease, whooping cough, Hib, and influenza.
- Breastfeed babies (ideally beyond four months) to boost their immune system.
- Don't smoke, and keep your home smoke-free.
- Keep your home warm and well-ventilated.
- Wash your hands regularly and thoroughly.
- Avoid close contact with people who have colds, flu, or other infections.
