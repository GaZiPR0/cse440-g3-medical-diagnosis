# 🔥 Gastritis

> **In short:** Gastritis is inflammation of the stomach lining. Most often caused by a bacterial infection, painkiller overuse, or heavy alcohol use — and for most people it improves quickly with treatment.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Category** | Stomach / digestive |
| **Common cause** | H. pylori bacteria |
| **Two forms** | Acute and chronic |
| **Outlook** | Usually improves with care |

---

## Overview

**Gastritis** is a general term for a group of conditions that share one thing: inflammation of the lining of the stomach. It is most often caused by infection with **H. pylori** — the same bacterium behind most stomach ulcers. Regular use of certain pain relievers and drinking too much alcohol can also contribute.

Gastritis may come on suddenly (**acute gastritis**) or develop slowly over time (**chronic gastritis**). In some cases it can lead to ulcers and a raised risk of stomach cancer, but for most people it isn't serious and improves quickly with treatment.

## Symptoms

- Upper abdominal pain (a gnawing or burning ache)
- Nausea and vomiting
- Feeling full soon after eating
- Bloating
- Loss of appetite

Some people have no symptoms at all. Pain may ease or worsen with eating.

## Diagnosis

Your doctor may suspect gastritis after discussing your history and examining you, and may confirm the cause with one or more tests:

- **Tests for H. pylori** — the bacterium can be detected by a blood, stool, or breath test. In the breath test, you drink a clear liquid; if H. pylori is present, your breath sample will contain a marker it produces.
- **Endoscopy** — a thin, flexible tube with a lens is passed down the throat to look for inflammation. Small tissue samples (a biopsy) may be taken to examine in the lab.
- **Upper GI X-ray (barium swallow)** — a series of X-rays of the oesophagus, stomach, and small intestine after swallowing a chalky barium liquid that makes problems easier to see.

## Treatment

Treatment depends on the cause. Acute gastritis triggered by NSAIDs or alcohol often eases simply by stopping those substances. Medicines used to treat gastritis include:

- **Antibiotics** — to kill H. pylori, usually a combination taken for 7–14 days. Complete the full course.
- **Proton pump inhibitors (PPIs)** — such as omeprazole and esomeprazole, which block acid production and promote healing.
- **Acid blockers (H-2 blockers)** — such as famotidine, which reduce the acid released into the digestive tract.
- **Antacids** — neutralise existing stomach acid for rapid pain relief.

> ℹ️ **A note on long-term PPI use.** Taking proton pump inhibitors long-term, especially at high doses, may raise the risk of hip, wrist, and spine fractures. Ask your doctor whether a calcium supplement would help.

## Prevention

It isn't fully clear how H. pylori spreads, but it may pass from person to person or through contaminated food and water. To protect yourself:

- Wash your hands often with soap and water.
- Eat foods that have been cooked thoroughly.
- Limit alcohol and avoid frequent use of NSAID painkillers.
