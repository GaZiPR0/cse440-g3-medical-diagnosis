# ⚖️ Obesity

> **In short:** Obesity is a chronic condition marked by an excess amount of body fat. It raises the risk of many other health problems, but even modest, steady weight loss brings real benefits.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Category** | Chronic metabolic condition |
| **Measured by** | Body Mass Index (BMI) |
| **Obese BMI** | 30 or above |
| **Manageable?** | Yes — with lifestyle change |

---

## Overview

**Obesity** is a chronic condition defined by an excess amount of body fat. Some body fat is essential — for storing energy, insulating against heat, and cushioning the body — but too much harms health. Obesity is best described using **Body Mass Index (BMI)**, which compares weight to height and correlates strongly with total body fat in adults.

| BMI range | Category |
|---|---|
| 18.5 – 24.9 | Normal weight |
| 25.0 – 29.9 | Overweight |
| 30.0 and above | Obesity |
| Over 40 | Severe (morbid) obesity |

## Diagnosis

BMI is the starting point. In children, the doctor plots BMI on a growth chart to find the percentile compared with others of the same age and sex — the 85th–94th percentile indicates overweight, and the 95th or above indicates obesity. Because BMI doesn't account for muscle or body frame, doctors also weigh up family history, eating and activity habits, other health conditions, and psychosocial factors.

**Blood tests** — If obesity is found, the doctor may order a cholesterol test, a blood sugar test, and other tests to check for hormone imbalances, vitamin D deficiency, or related conditions. Some of these require fasting beforehand.

## Treatment

The goal is to reach and stay at a healthier weight, which improves overall health and lowers the risk of complications. Losing even **5–10%** of your body weight can begin to improve your health. Steady, gradual weight loss is safest and most likely to last.

**Dietary changes**

- **Cut calories** — a typical target is 1,200–1,500 calories a day for women and 1,500–1,800 for men.
- **Feel full on less** — larger portions of low-calorie foods like fruit and vegetables satisfy hunger with fewer calories.
- **Choose healthier foods** — more plant foods, whole grains, and lean protein; less salt, added sugar, and unhealthy fat.
- Be wary of crash diets and fad diets — the weight usually returns.

**Exercise & activity** — Aim for at least 150 minutes a week of moderate activity to prevent further gain, and 300 minutes or more for significant loss. Everyday movement counts too — a goal of around 10,000 steps a day is a good target to build up to.

**Behaviour changes & support** — Counselling and support groups help you understand eating triggers, cope with cravings, and make lasting lifestyle changes.

> ℹ️ **Medication & procedures.** When diet and exercise aren't enough, a doctor may consider prescription weight-loss medication (for a BMI of 30+, or 27+ with complications), endoscopic procedures, or bariatric (weight-loss) surgery — always alongside lifestyle changes, not instead of them.
