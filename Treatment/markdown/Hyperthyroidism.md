# ⚡ Hyperthyroidism

> **In short:** An overactive thyroid gland making too much hormone, which speeds the whole body up — weight loss, a racing heart, tremor and restlessness.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Gland involved** | Thyroid (front of neck) |
| **Common cause** | Graves' disease |
| **Key sign** | Weight loss with good appetite |
| **Key test** | TSH blood test |

---

## Overview

The **thyroid** is a small butterfly-shaped gland at the front of the neck. The hormone it makes sets the pace of the body's metabolism — how fast you burn energy, how fast your heart beats, how warm you feel.

In **hyperthyroidism** the gland makes too much of that hormone and everything runs too fast. This is the opposite of hypothyroidism, where too little is made and everything slows down. The most common cause is **Graves' disease**, in which the immune system stimulates the gland; thyroid nodules and inflammation of the gland can also be responsible. It is considerably more common in women.

## Symptoms

- Weight loss despite eating the same amount or more
- A fast, pounding or irregular heartbeat
- Feeling hot all the time and not tolerating warm weather
- Sweating far more than before
- A fine trembling of the hands and fingers
- Nervousness, anxiety and irritability
- Difficulty falling asleep
- More frequent bowel movements
- Muscle weakness, especially in the thighs and upper arms
- Tiredness despite feeling restless
- A swelling at the base of the neck (goitre)
- Bulging or gritty, irritated eyes, in Graves' disease

> ⚠️ **Thyroid storm — a medical emergency:** very rarely, symptoms escalate suddenly into a high fever, a very fast heartbeat, agitation and confusion. This needs emergency hospital care immediately.

## Diagnosis

A **blood test** settles the diagnosis. A low **TSH** with a raised **T3 and T4** confirms an overactive thyroid. Antibody tests can identify Graves' disease as the cause.

A **thyroid scan** or ultrasound shows how the gland is working and whether nodules are responsible, which guides the choice of treatment.

## Treatment

Hyperthyroidism is very treatable. Which route suits you depends on the cause, your age and whether you are pregnant — an endocrinologist will discuss the options.

- **Anti-thyroid medicines** such as carbimazole or methimazole, which reduce hormone production over weeks to months
- **Beta blockers** to settle the racing heart, tremor and anxiety quickly while other treatment takes effect
- **Radioactive iodine**, taken as a drink or capsule, which shrinks the overactive gland
- **Surgery** to remove part or all of the thyroid, when other treatments are unsuitable

> 💡 **A common outcome:** after radioactive iodine or surgery the thyroid often becomes underactive. That is expected and easily managed with a daily thyroid hormone tablet — it is not a treatment failure.

### Living with it

- Take medicines exactly as prescribed and keep every follow-up blood test
- Report a sore throat, mouth ulcers or fever while on anti-thyroid medicines at once — rarely they affect the white blood cells
- Limit caffeine, which worsens palpitations and restlessness
- Eat enough to make up for the raised metabolism
- Protect the eyes with sunglasses and lubricating drops if they are affected

## Living well

- Do not stop treatment because you feel better — hormone levels rebound
- Keep a note of your weight, pulse and symptoms between appointments
- Tell any doctor treating you that you have a thyroid condition
- Ask before taking iodine-rich supplements or seaweed products
- Speak to your doctor before planning a pregnancy, as treatment needs adjusting
