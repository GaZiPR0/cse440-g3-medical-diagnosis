# 🚽 Gastroenteritis

> **In short:** Inflammation of the stomach and intestines from an infection, bringing watery diarrhoea, vomiting and cramps. The real danger is dehydration.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Caused by** | Viruses, bacteria, parasites |
| **Key sign** | Watery diarrhoea |
| **Main risk** | Dehydration |
| **Mainstay** | Oral rehydration solution |

---

## Overview

**Gastroenteritis** is inflammation of the stomach and intestines, usually from an infection picked up in contaminated food or water, or passed on by someone who is already ill. It is often called *stomach flu*, though it has nothing to do with influenza.

Viruses such as norovirus and rotavirus are the most common cause. Bacteria — including *Salmonella*, *Shigella*, *Campylobacter* and some strains of *E. coli* — cause what is usually called food poisoning, which often starts within hours of a suspect meal. Most people recover in a few days. The real danger, especially for small children and older adults, is losing fluid faster than it is replaced.

## Symptoms

- Watery diarrhoea, several times a day
- Cramping pain in the stomach
- Nausea and repeated vomiting
- A mild fever with chills
- Mild muscle aches and headache
- Weakness, thirst and a dry mouth from fluid loss

### Warning signs of dehydration

- Passing much less urine, or dark urine
- A dry mouth and tongue, and no tears when crying
- Sunken eyes; in a baby, a sunken soft spot on the head
- Dizziness on standing, drowsiness or confusion

> ⚠️ **Go to a doctor if:** there is blood or mucus in the stool, a high fever, severe stomach pain, vomiting that prevents you keeping any fluid down, diarrhoea lasting more than two or three days, or any sign of dehydration — especially in a baby or an older person.

## Diagnosis

Gastroenteritis is usually recognised from the symptoms and a recent history of suspect food or contact with someone ill. A **stool test** is done when symptoms are severe or prolonged, when there is blood in the stool, or when a parasite is suspected, so the right treatment can be chosen.

## Treatment

The single most important treatment is **replacing lost fluid and salts**. Most cases are viral, and antibiotics neither help nor shorten them — they are used only for specific bacterial or parasitic infections identified by a doctor.

### Rehydration comes first

- Take **oral rehydration solution (ORS)** in small, frequent sips — this is the mainstay
- Keep drinking even if you vomit; small sips every few minutes are kept down better
- Plain water alone does not replace lost salts; ORS does
- A child should be offered ORS after every loose stool

### Eating again

- Return to food as soon as you can manage it — starving does not help
- Start with plain, bland food: rice, bananas, toast, khichuri, boiled potato
- Avoid fatty, fried, very spicy or very sugary food for a few days
- Milk and dairy may be poorly tolerated for a short while

> ⚠️ **About anti-diarrhoeal medicines:** do not take loperamide-type medicines if there is a high fever or blood in the stool, and never give them to young children. They trap the infection inside. Zinc supplements, however, are recommended for children with diarrhoea.

## Prevention

- Wash hands with soap before eating and after using the toilet
- Drink boiled, filtered or sealed bottled water
- Eat food that is freshly cooked and thoroughly heated
- Keep raw and cooked food separate, and refrigerate leftovers promptly
- Wash fruit and vegetables in safe water; peel them where you can
- Stay away from preparing food for others while you are ill and for two days after
