🦟 Overview

Malaria is a disease caused by a parasite that is passed to humans through the bites of infected mosquitoes. People with malaria usually feel very unwell, with a high fever and shaking chills. Each year it infects around 210 million people and causes about 440,000 deaths — most of them young children in Africa.

Malaria is uncommon in temperate climates but remains widespread in tropical and subtropical countries. If you're travelling to a region where malaria is common, take steps to avoid mosquito bites and ask about preventive medicine — many malaria parasites are now resistant to the most common drugs.

⚠️ Seek medical attention early Malaria can become severe very quickly. See a doctor as soon as possible if you develop fever during or after travel to a malaria area.

🔍 Diagnosis

To diagnose malaria, a doctor reviews your medical history and travel, examines you, and orders blood tests — the only way to confirm the disease. Blood tests can show:

  • The presence of the parasite, confirming malaria
  • Which type of malaria parasite is involved
  • Whether the parasite is resistant to certain drugs

Other blood tests check for serious complications. Some results are ready in under 15 minutes, while others take several days.

💊 Treatment

Treatment aims to clear the Plasmodium parasite from the bloodstream. Even people without symptoms may be treated to reduce the risk of spreading the infection to others.

For uncomplicated malaria, the World Health Organization recommends artemisinin-based combination therapy (ACT). Artemisinin, derived from the sweet wormwood plant, rapidly lowers the number of parasites in the blood, while a partner drug clears the rest. Where the parasite is resistant to ACT, treatment must include an effective partner drug.

Expanding access to ACT has greatly reduced malaria's impact, but resistance is a growing concern, and no strong alternative to artemisinin is expected for several years.

🛡️ Prevention

  • Sleep under an insecticide-treated mosquito net.
  • Use insect repellent and wear protective, covering clothing.
  • Take preventive antimalarial medicine before, during, and after travel if advised.
  • Remove standing water and use screens to keep mosquitoes out.
ℹ️ Vaccines Research into safe, effective malaria vaccines is ongoing, and one vaccine has been licensed in Europe. Seek medical attention as early as possible for any suspected malaria symptoms.
