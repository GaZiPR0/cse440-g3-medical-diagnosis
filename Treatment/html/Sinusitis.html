Sinus Inflammation · “Sinus Infection”

Sinusitis

Swelling of the air-filled spaces behind the face, which blocks them and traps mucus. It causes facial pressure, a blocked nose and thick discharge.

Affects
Sinuses around nose & eyes
Key sign
Facial pain and pressure
Common cause
Virus after a cold
Most cases
Clear without antibiotics

🔎 Overview

The sinuses are hollow spaces in the bones of the face, around the nose, cheeks and forehead. They are lined with a thin membrane that makes mucus, which normally drains freely into the nose.

In sinusitis that lining becomes swollen. The drainage openings narrow, mucus is trapped, and pressure builds — which is why the pain sits behind the cheeks and forehead and gets worse when you bend forward. Most cases follow a common cold and are caused by a virus. Sinusitis is called acute when it lasts under four weeks and chronic when it drags on beyond twelve.

🩺 Symptoms

  • Pain, pressure or fullness around the cheeks, eyes or forehead
  • Pain that worsens on bending forward or lying down
  • Thick yellow or green discharge from the nose, or down the throat
  • A blocked, stuffy nose
  • Reduced or lost sense of smell
  • Pain in the upper teeth or jaw
  • Bad breath, and a headache that is worse in the morning
  • A mild fever and tiredness
See a doctor urgently if you have:swelling or redness around the eye, double or reduced vision, a severe headache with a stiff neck, confusion, or a high fever. These are rare but serious.

🧪 Diagnosis

Most sinusitis is diagnosed from the symptoms alone. A doctor will press over the cheeks and forehead to check for tenderness and look inside the nose for swelling and discharge.

Imaging such as a CT scan, or a look inside with a nasal endoscope, is reserved for chronic cases, repeated infections, or when a complication is suspected. Allergy testing may help if attacks keep coming back.

💊 Treatment

Because most cases are viral, antibiotics usually do not help and are only prescribed when symptoms are severe, last beyond about ten days, or improve and then get worse again. The aim of treatment is to open the sinuses and let them drain.

What helps

  • Saline nasal rinses — the single most useful measure; use boiled and cooled or distilled water
  • Steam inhalation, or a warm shower, to loosen mucus
  • A warm compress over the cheeks and forehead
  • Paracetamol or ibuprofen for pain and fever
  • Decongestant sprays for no more than three days — longer use makes congestion worse
  • Steroid nasal sprays, especially where allergy plays a part
  • Drinking plenty of fluids, and sleeping with the head raised

For chronic sinusitis

Long-lasting sinusitis is managed with steroid sprays, treatment of any underlying allergy, and sometimes surgery to widen the drainage openings if medicines are not enough.

🛡️ Prevention

  • Treat colds and allergies early, before congestion sets in
  • Wash hands often during cold and flu season
  • Avoid cigarette smoke and dusty, polluted air
  • Use a humidifier in very dry rooms
  • Manage hay fever and dust allergy with your doctor's advice
