🦋 Overview

Hypothyroidism (an underactive thyroid) is a condition in which the thyroid gland — a small, butterfly-shaped gland in the neck — doesn't produce enough of certain crucial hormones. These hormones control how the body uses energy, so when they run low, many body processes slow down.

🩺 Symptoms

TirednessWeight gainDry skin ConstipationFeeling coldSlowed heart rate Low mood

Symptoms often develop slowly over years and can be easy to miss. A doctor may test for it if you feel increasingly tired, have dry skin, constipation and weight gain, or have had previous thyroid problems or a goitre.

🔍 Diagnosis

Diagnosis is based on your symptoms and blood tests.

  • TSH test — the best screening test. A high level of TSH suggests an underactive thyroid, because the pituitary gland makes extra TSH to try to stimulate the thyroid.
  • Thyroxine (T4) test — a low level of this thyroid hormone, alongside high TSH, confirms hypothyroidism.

TSH testing also helps set and adjust your medication dose over time, and can detect subclinical hypothyroidism — raised TSH with still-normal hormone levels and few or no symptoms.

ℹ️ Before your blood test Some substances can affect thyroid blood tests, including the blood thinner heparin and the vitamin biotin (found in some supplements). Tell your doctor about any medicines or supplements you take.

💊 Treatment

Hypothyroidism is treated with a daily dose of synthetic thyroid hormone, which restores normal hormone levels and reverses the symptoms. The right dose is found and fine-tuned using regular TSH tests, and treatment is usually lifelong.

Why the thyroid can become underactive

  • Surgery — if the whole thyroid is removed, lifelong thyroid medication is needed; if only part is removed, the rest may still make enough hormone.
  • Radiation therapy — treatment for head/neck cancer, lymphoma, or leukaemia can slow or stop hormone production.
  • Certain medicines — some drugs used for psychiatric, heart, or cancer conditions can lower thyroid hormone production.
  • Overtreatment of an overactive thyroid — treating hyperthyroidism (for example with radioactive iodine) can sometimes leave hormone levels permanently low.
