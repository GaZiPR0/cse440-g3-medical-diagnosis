Hepatitis

Inflammation of the liver. It has many causes — most commonly the hepatitis viruses A, B, C, D, and E — and ranges from a short-lived illness to a long-term disease that can damage the liver.

🫀 Overview

Hepatitis means "inflammation of the liver." A liver can become inflamed for many reasons — too much alcohol, physical injury, an autoimmune response, or a reaction to bacteria or a virus. The five most common hepatitis viruses are A, B, C, D, and E. Some can lead to fibrosis, cirrhosis, liver failure, or even liver cancer. Because the liver filters toxins from the body, damage to it reduces how well the body can function.

🩺 Symptoms

Jaundice (yellow skin/eyes)FatigueNausea & vomiting Abdominal painDark urineLoss of appetite

Some forms, especially early on, cause no symptoms at all and are found only through blood tests.

🔍 How Hepatitis Is Diagnosed

  • History & physical exam — the doctor checks for risk factors, gently presses the abdomen for tenderness or an enlarged liver, and looks for yellowing of the skin or eyes.
  • Liver function tests — blood tests that reveal how well the liver is working; high liver-enzyme levels can be the first sign of a problem.
  • Other blood tests — identify the specific hepatitis virus or the antibodies seen in autoimmune hepatitis.
  • Ultrasound — creates an image of the liver and nearby organs, revealing fluid, damage, enlargement, or tumours.
  • Liver biopsy — a small tissue sample (taken with a needle, guided by ultrasound) shows how infection or inflammation has affected the liver.

💊 Treatment by Type

Treatment depends on which type of hepatitis you have and whether it is acute or chronic.

TypeTreatment & notes
Hepatitis AUsually needs no treatment — a short-term illness. Rest and hydration help. A vaccine prevents it.
Hepatitis BAcute B needs no specific treatment; chronic B is treated with antiviral medicines and monitoring. A vaccine prevents it (recommended for all newborns).
Hepatitis CTreated with antiviral drug combinations. Severe cases with cirrhosis may need a liver transplant. No vaccine yet.
Hepatitis DOnly occurs alongside hepatitis B. Preventing B (by vaccination) prevents D.
Hepatitis EUsually acute and clears on its own with rest, fluids, and good nutrition; pregnant women need close monitoring.
AutoimmuneTreated with corticosteroids (e.g. prednisone) and immune-suppressing drugs such as azathioprine.

🛡️ Prevention

Hygiene (types A & E)

When travelling in areas with poor sanitation, avoid local tap water, ice, raw or undercooked shellfish, and unwashed raw fruit and vegetables.

Avoiding blood-borne spread (types B, C, D)

  • Don't share needles, razors, or toothbrushes.
  • Avoid contact with spilled blood.
  • Practise safe sex, since B and C can spread through sexual contact.
ℹ️ Vaccines Safe, effective vaccines exist for hepatitis A and B (and B protects against D). Vaccination is one of the most important ways to prevent hepatitis.
