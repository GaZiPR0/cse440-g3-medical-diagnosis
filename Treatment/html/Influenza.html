🤒 Overview

Influenza is a viral infection that attacks your respiratory system — your nose, throat, and lungs. It's commonly called the flu, but it isn't the same as the stomach "flu" viruses that cause diarrhoea and vomiting. For most people, influenza clears up on its own, but sometimes it — and its complications — can be dangerous.

Although the yearly flu vaccine isn't 100% effective, it's still your best defence against the illness.

🩺 Symptoms

Fever & chillsMuscle achesHeadache Dry coughSore throatFatigue Runny or stuffy nose

Flu symptoms tend to come on suddenly and can leave you feeling far more unwell than a common cold.

⚠️ Who Is at Higher Risk

  • Young children under 5, especially under 12 months
  • Adults over 65
  • Residents of nursing homes and long-term care facilities
  • Pregnant women and women up to two weeks after birth
  • People with weakened immune systems
  • People with chronic illness — asthma, heart, kidney, or liver disease, and diabetes
  • People who are very obese (BMI of 40 or higher)

🔍 Diagnosis

Your doctor will do a physical exam and look for signs of influenza. When flu is widespread, a diagnosis is often made from symptoms alone. If a test is needed, PCR testing is increasingly common — it is more sensitive than older tests and can identify the specific influenza strain.

💊 Treatment

Usually, all you need is bed rest and plenty of fluids. For severe infection or higher-risk people, a doctor may prescribe an antiviral such as oseltamivir (Tamiflu), zanamivir (Relenza), peramivir (Rapivab), or baloxavir (Xofluza). Started early, these can shorten the illness by about a day and help prevent serious complications. (Zanamivir is inhaled and isn't suitable for people with asthma or other chronic lung disease.)

Home care

  • Drink plenty of fluids — water, juice, and warm soups to prevent dehydration.
  • Rest — extra sleep helps your immune system fight the infection.
  • Pain relievers — acetaminophen or ibuprofen ease aches and fever.
⚠️ Important for children Children and teens recovering from flu-like symptoms should never take aspirin, because of the risk of Reye's syndrome — a rare but potentially fatal condition.

🛡️ Prevention

  • Get the annual flu vaccine — your best protection.
  • Wash your hands often, and cover coughs and sneezes.
  • Stay home when sick, and keep sick children home until fever-free for 24 hours.
  • Avoid close contact with people who are ill.
