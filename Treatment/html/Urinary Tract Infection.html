Urinary Tract Infection

A bacterial infection of the bladder, urethra or kidneys. It burns when you urinate and drives a constant urge to go, and it clears quickly with antibiotics.

Usually caused by
E. coli bacteria
Key sign
Burning on urination
More common in
Women
Treatment
Antibiotics

🔎 Overview

A urinary tract infection (UTI) happens when bacteria — most often E. coli from the bowel — enter the urinary system and multiply. An infection of the bladder is called cystitis and is the common form. If bacteria travel further up to the kidneys the infection becomes pyelonephritis, which is far more serious.

UTIs are much more common in women, because the urethra is shorter and sits closer to the anus, giving bacteria a shorter journey. Pregnancy, diabetes, kidney stones, a urinary catheter and an enlarged prostate all raise the risk.

🩺 Symptoms

  • A burning or stinging feeling while urinating
  • Needing to urinate very often, with only a small amount each time
  • A constant urge to go that does not settle after urinating
  • Cloudy, dark or strong-smelling urine
  • Blood in the urine, or a pink tinge
  • Pain or pressure low in the abdomen
  • Fever, with pain in the lower back or side if the kidneys are involved
Signs the kidneys are involved — see a doctor the same day:fever with chills and shaking, pain in the back or side below the ribs, nausea and vomiting. A kidney infection needs prompt treatment. Confusion in an older person can also be the only sign of a UTI.

🧪 Diagnosis

A urine dipstick test gives a quick answer in the clinic by detecting white cells and nitrites. A urine culture identifies exactly which bacterium is responsible and which antibiotics will kill it — useful when infections keep returning or the first treatment does not work.

For repeated infections, or infection in men and children, a doctor may arrange an ultrasound or other imaging to look for a stone, a blockage or another underlying cause.

💊 Treatment

UTIs are treated with antibiotics prescribed by a doctor. A simple bladder infection often settles within a couple of days of starting them, but the full course must be completed — stopping early lets the surviving bacteria return, often harder to treat.

Alongside the antibiotics

  • Drink plenty of water to flush the bladder
  • Urinate as soon as you feel the urge; do not hold on
  • A warm pad on the lower abdomen eases the ache
  • Paracetamol for pain and fever
  • Avoid caffeine, alcohol and spicy food while symptoms last — they irritate the bladder
If you are pregnant:tell your doctor straight away. UTIs in pregnancy are treated promptly and with antibiotics chosen to be safe for the baby.

🛡️ Prevention

  • Drink enough water through the day
  • Do not hold urine for long periods
  • Wipe from front to back after using the toilet
  • Urinate soon after sex
  • Choose cotton underwear and avoid tight clothing
  • Avoid harsh soaps, douches and scented products in the genital area
