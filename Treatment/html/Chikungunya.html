Mosquito-Borne Viral Illness

Chikungunya

A mosquito-borne virus that arrives with sudden fever and severe, swollen joints. It is rarely fatal, but the joint pain can linger for months.

Spread by
Aedes mosquitoes
Key sign
Severe joint pain
Onset
Sudden, with fever
Specific cure?
None — supportive care

🔎 Overview

Chikungunya (say: chik-un-GUN-ya) is a viral infection carried by the same Aedes mosquitoes that spread dengue — the ones that bite during the day. The name comes from a word meaning “to become contorted”, describing the stooped posture of people bent over by joint pain.

Symptoms usually appear four to eight days after the bite. Almost everyone infected develops symptoms, which sets it apart from dengue. Deaths are very rare, but the joint pain is the defining feature: it can be disabling for weeks and, in some people, continues for months after the fever has gone.

🩺 Symptoms

  • Sudden high fever, starting at the same time as the joint pain
  • Severe pain and swelling in the wrists, ankles, fingers and knees
  • Joint stiffness that is worst in the morning
  • Rash on the trunk and limbs a few days after the fever begins
  • Headache with muscle pain
  • Deep tiredness that outlasts the fever
Chikungunya or dengue?Both bring high fever and body ache. Chikungunya is marked by swollen joints and severe joint stiffness; dengue is marked by pain behind the eyes, a falling platelet count and a risk of bleeding. Only a blood test can tell them apart with certainty.
Get medical help if:you have bleeding gums or nose, persistent vomiting, severe stomach pain, breathlessness, or you are elderly, pregnant, or have a long-term illness such as diabetes or heart disease.

🧪 Diagnosis

A doctor will suspect chikungunya from the combination of sudden fever and severely painful, swollen joints, particularly during an outbreak. Blood tests — RT-PCR in the first week, or antibody (IgM) tests after that — confirm it and rule out dengue, which is important because the two are managed differently.

💊 Treatment

There is no medicine that kills the chikungunya virus. Care is aimed at easing symptoms while the body clears the infection.

  • Rest, and drink plenty of fluids
  • Take paracetamol (acetaminophen) for fever and pain
  • Apply cold compresses to swollen joints
  • Move joints gently through their range once the worst pain settles, to prevent stiffness
Avoid aspirin and ibuprofen at first:until dengue has been ruled out, do not take aspirin, ibuprofen or naproxen — they increase bleeding risk. Your doctor may add an anti-inflammatory later, once dengue is excluded and if joint pain persists.

When joint pain lingers

In some people the joint pain continues for weeks or months. Gentle exercise, physiotherapy and medicines prescribed by a doctor help. Do not stop moving — complete rest makes stiffness worse.

🛡️ Prevention

  • Empty standing water from buckets, tyres, flowerpots and coolers every few days
  • Use insect repellent on exposed skin during the day
  • Wear long sleeves and trousers
  • Fit screens on windows and doors; sleep under a net
  • If you are ill, stay under a net for the first week so mosquitoes do not carry the virus onward
